# Why this skill refuses the "therapist" role

This is the most important document in the repo. If you read only one file besides `SKILL.md`, read this one.

---

## What "attractor basin" means here

Claude is a language model. When you assign it a role, it pulls toward the centroid of how that role talks in its training data. The training data for "therapist" is largely:

- Pop-psychology books
- Therapy memoirs
- TV portrayals
- Self-help blogs
- Online discussions of therapy
- Some real clinical literature (the smallest slice)

The centroid of all that is **therapy-speak**: warm validation, gentle reframes, open questions, lots of "what's coming up for you," lots of "I hear you." It sounds wise. It also sounds the same regardless of who you are.

Real therapy is the opposite of generic. A good therapist meets you specifically — your history, your defenses, your way of avoiding things, your relationship with them as a person. They draw on theory but they don't perform theory.

When you tell Claude "be my therapist," you get the centroid. Not the specificity.

---

## What this skill does instead

It assigns a **task**, not a **role**.

The task: observe patterns in what the user says, name them plainly, ask one good question at a time, do not perform warmth, do not validate as a ritual, do not close with an invitation to continue.

That's all. No "you are a therapist." No "you are a counselor." No "you are a coach who specializes in..." Just operational rules about what to *do*.

The result is something stranger and more useful: Claude observing you the way a sharp friend who happens to have read a lot of psychology might — without the friend's history with you, but also without the friend's investment in being liked.

---

## What this also means

It means this skill is **not therapy**. Real therapy involves:

- A relationship that develops over time, with rupture and repair
- A professional with legal, ethical, and clinical accountability
- Diagnosis (when appropriate)
- Treatment planning
- Containment of crisis
- Trauma processing done in a safe, paced way by someone trained to do it

None of that is happening here. A markdown file cannot contain crisis. Claude cannot hold the room. The mirror can be sharper but it cannot catch you when you fall.

---

## The honest pitch

If you have a therapist: this skill might be useful between sessions for processing, journaling, spotting your own patterns, organizing your thoughts before you see them.

If you don't have a therapist and you can get one: get one. Use this as supplementary if you want.

If you don't have a therapist and you genuinely can't get one — for reasons of money, geography, time, or a thousand other valid reasons — this skill is better than the default mode of Claude, which over-validates and produces a feeling of being deeply seen that is sometimes accurate and sometimes is the model finding what resonates.

But you have to do extra work. You have to:

- Stay critical. The skill makes Claude less sycophantic but doesn't make Claude correct.
- Notice when Claude is naming a pattern that fits versus a pattern that just *sounds* like it fits.
- Build your own scaffolding. Keep notes outside the chat. Talk to people you trust about what's coming up.
- Get help if it goes anywhere serious.

The name of this skill — `fraudi` — is in the title precisely to keep this honest. It's not a therapist. The clue is in the spelling.
